# The Recorder

The Recorder writes down a meeting while it happens: what you say into your microphone, and what the others say through your computer — a call in Zoom, Google Meet, Teams or any other app, or a video playing in your browser. Nobody joins the call; Aeven listens on your own computer.

The Recorder is part of the desktop app, on macOS, Windows and Linux. On Aeven Web, choosing it offers to continue in the desktop app. [Your desktop from Aeven Web](/docs/using/desktop-from-web)

## Opening it

Open the right sidebar and choose **Recorder**. A conversation has one Recorder; choosing it again brings back the one that is open.

The first time, Aeven downloads what it needs to tell voices apart, with a progress bar at the top of the Recorder. It takes a few seconds and happens once.

## Recording

Choose **Start recording**. The first recording asks for two permissions:

- **Microphone** — for what you say.
- **System audio recording** (macOS) — for what your computer plays, which is how Aeven hears the other people in the call.

While it records, the Recorder shows the time, a level for **You** and for **Others**, and the transcript as it is written, a few sentences at a time:

| Label | Who it is |
| --- | --- |
| You | Your microphone |
| Speaker 1, Speaker 2, … | The other voices, numbered in the order they first speak |

The buttons underneath add the transcript so far to the conversation, stop the recording, and discard it.

Aeven works out the meeting's language from the first stretch of speech and keeps to it; lines written before it was sure are corrected. Recording keeps running when you switch conversations, and closing the Recorder stops it.

## When it finishes

Choose **Stop**. Aeven finishes the last sentences, then the transcript stays in the Recorder with three buttons: **Copy**, **New recording**, and **Add to chat**, which puts the transcript into the message box so you can ask about it — "summarize this", "list the action items", "draft the follow-up email". [Chat and attachments](/docs/using/chat-and-attachments)

## Saved recordings

A recording in which something was said is saved automatically when it finishes, transcript and audio together; **Saved** appears next to the time. A recording you discard, or one with nothing said, is not kept.

The list button at the bottom of the Recorder opens your recordings, newest first. Open one to:

- play its audio,
- read the transcript,
- rename it — click the title and type,
- copy it or add it to the conversation,
- delete it — this removes the transcript and the audio on all your devices.

Your recordings follow your account: the transcripts appear on every computer you are signed in on, and the audio follows to your other computers, kept encrypted while it travels. Until the audio has arrived, a recording opened on another computer says so and shows the transcript.

## Transcription

The Recorder transcribes with the same voice model as dictation. Choose it in **Settings → General → Voice input**: on your own computer, so the audio never leaves it, or through Aeven. If the Recorder says voice input is off or not ready, **Open voice settings** takes you there.

## Before you record

The other people in the call are not told that Aeven is transcribing. Recording a conversation can need everyone's consent where you are — say that you are taking notes before you start.

## When something is missing

- **Computer audio unavailable** — only your microphone is transcribed. On macOS this needs macOS 14.2 or later, and **System audio recording** allowed for Aeven in **System Settings → Privacy & Security**.
- **Your voice appears twice** — your speakers are loud enough for your microphone to pick up the call. Headphones fix it.
- **Everyone is one speaker, or one person is two** — voices that sound alike, or very short interjections, can be labelled wrongly. Longer turns are labelled reliably.
- **No speaker numbers, only Others** — telling voices apart is not available on this computer (Windows on ARM), or its download has not finished; check the top of the Recorder.
